## What Are Commodities?

Commodities are **raw materials and natural resources** that are standardized and interchangeable with other goods of the same type. They fall into several main categories:

### Precious Metals

- **Gold** - Traditional safe haven and inflation hedge
- **Silver** - Industrial and investment demand
- **Platinum** - Automotive and industrial applications
- **Palladium** - Critical for catalytic converters

### Energy

- **Crude Oil** - Global energy benchmark
- **Natural Gas** - Heating and electricity generation
- **Gasoline** - Refined petroleum products
- **Heating Oil** - Seasonal demand commodity

### Agriculture

- **Corn** - Food and ethanol production
- **Wheat** - Global food staple
- **Soybeans** - Food, feed, and biofuel
- **Coffee** - Global beverage commodity
- **Sugar** - Food and industrial uses
- **Cotton** - Textile manufacturing

### Industrial Metals

- **Copper** - Construction and electrical
- **Aluminum** - Transportation and packaging
- **Nickel** - Stainless steel and batteries
- **Zinc** - Galvanization and alloys

## Risk Management

### Commodity-Specific Risks

**High Volatility:**
Commodities can experience rapid, large price swings driven by:

- Weather events (agriculture)
- Geopolitical tensions (oil, gas)
- Economic data (industrial metals)
- Currency movements (dollar-denominated)

**Contango & Backwardation:**
Commodity ETFs using futures may experience:

- **Contango:** Future prices higher than spot (roll cost)
- **Backwardation:** Future prices lower than spot (roll yield)

**No Income:**
Unlike stocks (dividends) or bonds (interest), most commodities generate no income (except through price appreciation).

**Supply/Demand Shocks:**
Unexpected events can cause dramatic price changes:

- OPEC production cuts/increases
- Mine closures or discoveries
- Crop failures or bumper harvests
- Technological breakthroughs

### Risk Mitigation Strategies

- **Diversify** across commodity types
- **Limit allocation** to 5-15% of portfolio
- **Use ETFs** instead of futures (for most investors)
- **Dollar-cost average** into positions
- **Rebalance** regularly to maintain targets

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What's the best way to invest in commodities?**
A: For most investors, commission-free commodity ETFs offer the easiest and most cost-effective exposure.

**Q: How much of my portfolio should be in commodities?**
A: Financial advisors typically recommend 5-10% for diversification and inflation protection.

**Q: Can I hold commodities in my IRA?**
A: Yes! Commodity ETFs can be held in Traditional and Roth IRAs where supported by Bounding Curve.

**Q: Do commodities pay dividends?**
A: Most commodity ETFs don't pay dividends (price appreciation only). However, commodity stock ETFs may pay dividends.

**Q: When should I invest in gold?**
A: Gold often performs well during economic uncertainty, inflation, currency devaluation, and geopolitical tensions.

**Q: Are commodities risky?**
A: Yes, commodities can be volatile. Limit allocation, diversify across types, and use a long-term perspective.

**Q: What's the difference between commodity ETFs and futures?**
A: ETFs trade like stocks, have no expiration, and are easier for most investors. Futures require active management, have expiration dates, and use leverage.
